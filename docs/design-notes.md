# Point, tap, bloom

The live phone camera is the starting screen. The shutter is the only primary action. Visible text is limited to the Paint Ithaca wordmark, Retake, Flip and Submit actions, and necessary camera permission or error messages. There are no decorative captions, reference photos or privacy footer.

A tap freezes the visible frame and stops the camera. Graphite Blooms' delicate pencil contours appear across the image in 140 milliseconds. After a brief 100-millisecond lead-in, Tidal Bloom floods in with a feathery wash, using Prussian Sunprint’s irregular developing edge without its blue color treatment. The complete reveal takes 2.0 seconds. The pencil recedes beneath the color, leaving subtle shading in the finished photograph. The photo keeps its natural colors. The final frame offers Retake and Submit. Submit pulls the painting back into a large array of softly colored Ithaca photos, with sparse spatial points in the gaps suggesting a shared reconstruction. The thank-you message appears after joining. Capture another retains the mosaic until a live camera frame is ready, then crossfades back into the viewfinder. The camera icon stays visible throughout painting and gives way to Retake and Submit when it finishes. There is one effect and no pause button or effect selector.

The visible live preview is a canvas fed by an offscreen video, preventing native playback overlays. The shutter is a simple camera icon on a faint gradient with subtle grain. The viewfinder has a subtle 6px radius. A warm autumn gradient colors the wordmark. Fine grain and longer fibers give the cream background a rough paper surface; sage and lavender remain in the quiet controls. Native click and keyboard activation require no holding.

Camera permission is requested on startup. Device or permission problems appear inside the viewfinder with a retry action. Photos stay on the device and the microphone is never requested.
